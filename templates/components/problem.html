The average household wastes about a fifth of the food it buys, often before it's even used — roughly $200 a month for a typical family. That's not a willpower problem. It's a planning problem.

Eat Well, Spend Less was written to fix the planning, not the person. It's built around whole foods you likely already recognize — oats, beans, lentils, seasonal vegetables — arranged into a repeatable weekly system: a shopping list, a cooking order, and a way to track what you're actually saving.
